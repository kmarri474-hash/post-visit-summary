Patient: I have sumatriptan fifty milligrams. It helps about sixty percent of the time, if I catch it early. When it doesn't work I take naproxen and lie in a dark room.

Patient: Red wine. Poor sleep. Sometimes my period. Skipping meals. Screen time when I'm tired.

Dr. Thompson: Good. Your exam is normal — no focal neurological findings, fundoscopy is clear, no neck tenderness, temporal arteries non-tender. Let me go over what I think is happening and what we should do. You're describing classic migraine with typical aura — visual scintillations preceding a unilateral throbbing headache with photophobia, phonophobia, and nausea. The sensory aura in your hand adds a somatosensory component but is still consistent with migraine. Your three-per-month attacks and eight-to-ten total headache days put you in the "episodic migraine" category with clear indication for preventive therapy. My plan is four parts. One: prevention. I'm starting you on topiramate twenty-five milligrams at bedtime for one week, then fifty at bedtime, and we'll target seventy-five to a hundred milligrams daily if tolerated. It takes six to eight weeks for full effect. Side effects to know about: tingling in your hands or feet, change in taste especially with carbonated drinks, some word-finding difficulty, weight loss. Avoid pregnancy on it — use reliable contraception, which you already have. If topiramate doesn't work or you can't tolerate it, we'll try a CGRP antagonist — those are the monthly injectables — but insurance usually requires trying topiramate or propranolol first. Two: rescue. Continue sumatriptan fifty for acute attacks, but I'm also going to add naproxen as a co-medication — some studies show combining a triptan with an NSAID improves response. Take them together at the first sign of aura. I'd like to limit your triptan days to no more than eight per month and all acute meds combined to no more than ten days. Three: lifestyle — and I know you've heard this before. Consistent sleep schedule, hydration, don't skip meals, consider cutting red wine for eight weeks to see if it helps. I'll give you a headache diary app to track attacks and triggers. Four: red flags — if you get a headache that is truly the worst of your life, sudden onset, associated with fever, neck stiffness, weakness, confusion, or speech problems, go to the ER immediately. These wouldn't be migraines. Given your first-time sensory aura, I want to get a brain MRI without contrast — not because I expect to find anything, but to confirm a normal structural exam before we commit to long-term preventive therapy. Follow-up in six weeks to assess topiramate response. If we're not at fifty percent reduction by then, we'll escalate.
